# Policy Agenda Tracking

## Analytical scope
Distinguish:
- Public attention: what users discuss and when.
- Media agenda: what news outlets emphasize.
- Political/institutional agenda: what officials and agencies formally prioritize.
- Policy decisions: documented commitments, regulations, budgets, implementation, or enforcement.
These agendas can interact, but one is not proof of another.

## Workflow
1. Define the policy problem, jurisdiction, responsible institutions, target population, and period.
2. Build a dated policy chronology from primary documents: draft plans, regulations, meeting minutes, official statements, budgets, permits, implementation reports, and evaluation documents.
3. Build a digital issue chronology: keywords/hashtags, volume, actors, frames, events, and interaction patterns. Preserve query and collection metadata.
4. Align timelines. Identify precedence, lags, overlaps, and discontinuities. Do not assume that a social media spike caused institutional action.
5. Analyze agenda dynamics: issue emergence, amplification, framing contests, institutional acknowledgement, policy adoption, implementation, and feedback.
6. Trace mechanisms: public petition, direct consultation, media coverage, formal hearing, official reply, advocacy coalition, or other observable pathway.
7. Evaluate policy response against the documented policy instrument and implementation evidence; separate announcement from implementation and implementation from outcome.
8. Develop alternative explanations: electoral cycles, legal mandates, administrative routines, elite advocacy, media agenda, offline mobilization, crises, and pre-existing plans.
9. Present evidence confidence and unresolved gaps.

## Policy-cycle matrix
| Stage | Observable evidence | Data source | Inference limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem recognition | issue statements, complaints, reports | social data, reports | attention is not formal recognition |
| Agenda setting | meeting agendas, official acknowledgement | official records | temporal sequence is not causation |
| Formulation | drafts, consultations, options | policy documents | draft is not adopted policy |
| Adoption | signed regulation/decision/budget | primary legal/official source | adoption is not implementation |
| Implementation | permits, services, enforcement, spending | administrative data | activity is not outcome |
| Evaluation | monitoring, audit, outcome indicators | evaluation records | attribution requires design |

## Causal discipline
When evaluating policy influence, consider process tracing, comparative cases, interrupted time series, or other designs only where assumptions and data support them. State confounders and counterfactual limitations. Use language such as "coincided with", "was acknowledged by", or "is consistent with" unless causal evidence is stronger.

## Deliverable
Create an issue brief containing: policy context; public attention timeline; framing and actor map; institutional chronology; documented pathways between public discourse and policy; implementation evidence; competing explanations; conclusions; actionable recommendations; and source appendix.
